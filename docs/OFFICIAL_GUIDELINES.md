# 2025 BECE SCHOOL SELECTION GUIDELINES

## Document Purpose

This document contains the school-selection guidelines for the 2025 Basic Education Certificate Examination (BECE) candidates, based on the government guidelines and school-selection materials supplied for this project.

The application must use this document as the content source for its **Guidelines** section.

The purpose of the Guidelines section is to help candidates, parents, guardians, teachers, and school administrators understand the school-selection requirements before completing a selection form.

### Important Content Rules

1. Preserve the meaning of the official government instructions.
2. Do not silently change, remove, or weaken any official requirement.
3. Keep official instructions separate from explanations written to improve understanding.
4. Do not present an explanation as though it were official government wording.
5. If an instruction is unclear or conflicts with another instruction, flag it for verification rather than inventing a rule.
6. The guidelines displayed in the application must be readable on mobile phones and computers.
7. The interface may present the guidelines in interactive sections, cards, a step-by-step reader, or another accessible format, provided the official content remains accurate and complete.

# SECTION 1: UNDERSTANDING SCHOOL SELECTION

### Official Guideline 1: Select Five Main Schools

Candidates are required to select five (5) schools and rank them according to their order of preference.

The first choice should be the candidate's most preferred school.

### Explanation

You must provide five main school choices. Arrange them in the order you prefer, with Choice 1 representing your first preference, Choice 2 your second preference, and so on.

Choose carefully. According to the supplied 2025 guidelines, a candidate placed in any of the selected choices will not be changed.

**Application behaviour:** The application should display the five main choices clearly, numbered from Choice 1 to Choice 5. Candidates should be able to edit their choices before completing the review process.

---

### Official Guideline 2: Choose a Programme for Each School

Candidates must select a programme for each main school choice.

The selection form includes fields for the school code and name, category, programme code and name, and residential preference.

### Explanation

Selecting a school is not necessarily the end of the process. You must also select an eligible programme offered by that school.

The application should help you identify the programmes available at each school. It must not allow a candidate to select a programme that the school does not offer, provided the programme information has been verified.

**Application behaviour:** Once a school is selected, the application should present the relevant programme options supported by the verified data. If the programme information is missing or uncertain, the application must not invent an option.

---

# SECTION 2: SCHOOL CATEGORIES

### Official Guideline 3: Category A Limit

A candidate cannot select more than one school from Category A.

### Explanation

Your five main choices may include no more than one Category A school under the general selection rules.

For example, selecting one Category A school in Choice 1 means you cannot select another Category A school in Choices 2, 3, 4, or 5 under those same general rules.

**Application behaviour:** The application should count Category A selections automatically and prevent an additional selection when the applicable limit has been reached.

---

### Official Guideline 4: Category B Limit

A candidate cannot select more than two schools from Category B.

### Explanation

Under the general selection rules, you may select up to two Category B schools among your five main choices.

The supplied guidelines also contain a residential arrangement for Category B schools. See the residential preference section below.

**Application behaviour:** The application should count Category B schools among the main choices and enforce the applicable limit.

---

### Official Guideline 5: Category C Choices

Candidates may select all five schools from Category C, subject to the residential preference requirements.

### Explanation

The supplied guidelines permit candidates to choose all five main schools from Category C. The candidate must still follow the applicable Day and Boarding requirements.

Do not interpret permission to select five Category C schools as permission to ignore other selection requirements.

**Application behaviour:** The application must evaluate category limits and residential requirements together. It must not reject five Category C choices solely because all five belong to Category C.

---

# SECTION 3: DAY AND BOARDING PREFERENCES

### Official Guideline 6: Residential Preference for the Five Main Choices

Candidates must select three (3) Boarding schools and two (2) Day schools among their five main choices.

### Explanation

Your five main choices must satisfy the stated residential arrangement:

- Three Boarding selections.
- Two Day selections.

The residential preference is part of the school selection, not an optional detail that can be ignored.

A school may offer Day only, Boarding only, or both Day and Boarding. The available options must be determined from verified school information and the applicable selection rules.

**Application behaviour:**

1. The application should track the number of Day and Boarding selections.
2. It should show candidates how many selections of each type remain.
3. It should prevent a candidate from choosing an unavailable residential option.
4. It should identify a Day-only school as Day-only.
5. It should identify a Boarding-only school as Boarding-only.
6. Where a school offers both options, the candidate should be able to select the permitted option.
7. The application should validate the final arrangement before presenting the form as valid.

A school that offers only Day must not become selectable as Boarding simply because the candidate needs a Boarding choice.

Likewise, a school that offers only Boarding must not become selectable as Day.

---

### Official Guideline 7: Category B Residential Arrangement

Candidates may select one Category B school as Boarding and another Category B school as Day.

### Explanation

The supplied guidelines permit the two Category B choices to be arranged as one Boarding selection and one Day selection.

The category limit and residential arrangement must both be respected.

**Application behaviour:** Where two Category B schools are selected under the applicable general rules, the application should verify the required residential arrangement and the actual options available at each school.

---

### Official Guideline 8: Category C Residential Arrangement

Candidates may select one Category C school as Boarding and another Category C school as Day.

### Explanation

The supplied guidelines also describe a residential arrangement for Category C schools.

The application must consider this instruction alongside the stated permission to select all five main schools from Category C and the overall requirement for three Boarding and two Day selections.

The precise interaction between these instructions must be verified against the official source before it is implemented as a rigid validation rule.

**Application behaviour:** Do not implement contradictory or assumed restrictions. Apply the confirmed official rules and flag any unresolved interpretation for review.

---

# SECTION 4: ALTERNATIVE SCHOOL CHOICES

### Official Guideline 9: Select Two Alternative Schools

Candidates must select two (2) alternative schools:

- One Category B school.
- One Category C school.

The alternative schools must be selected from Appendix 3 of the supplied 2025 school-selection guidelines.

### Explanation

In addition to your five main choices, you must provide two alternative choices that meet the stated category requirements.

These are separate from the five main choices.

**Application behaviour:** The application should provide a clearly labelled Alternative Choices section with separate fields for the Category B and Category C alternatives.

Only schools verified as eligible under Appendix 3 should be offered in this section.

---

### Official Guideline 10: Alternative Schools Must Not Repeat Main Choices

Candidates cannot repeat a school from their five main choices among their two alternative choices.

### Explanation

An alternative choice must be a different school from each of your five main choices.

For example, if a school appears in your main choices, you must not select that same school again as an alternative.

**Application behaviour:** The application should compare alternative choices with the five main choices and prevent duplicate school selections across those sections.

This duplicate restriction must be applied to the school identity, not merely the spelling of its name.

---

### Official Guideline 11: Purpose of Alternative Choices

Alternative school placement may occur if a candidate is not placed in any of the five main choices.

### Explanation

The alternative choices provide another possible placement route when the candidate does not obtain placement through the five main choices.

They should not be presented as guaranteed placement.

The application must explain the purpose of alternative choices without promising that a candidate will be placed in either alternative school.

---

### Official Guideline 12: Placement Is Not Changed

The supplied 2025 guidelines state that a candidate placed in any of the selected choices will not be changed.

### Explanation

Candidates should understand the importance of reviewing their preferences before completing their selection.

The application should encourage careful review, but it must not independently reinterpret government placement policy or promise that an assigned placement can be changed.

# SECTION 5: SPECIAL SELECTION PATHWAYS

The supplied guidelines include special instructions for candidates selecting pure TVET programmes and candidates interested in STEM or Science.

These instructions must be handled carefully because they may affect how the general selection rules apply.

## 5.1 Pure TVET Programme Selection

### Official Guideline 13: Pure TVET Candidates

Candidates selecting pure TVET programmes are instructed to select all five TVET institutes from Categories A, B, and C as Day or Boarding, following the stated selection criteria.

### Explanation

A candidate following the pure TVET pathway should be guided towards eligible TVET institutes and the programmes offered by those institutions.

The application must distinguish a TVET institute from an ordinary senior high school and must verify the programmes available at each institution.

The residential preference must reflect the official information and applicable rules.

**Application behaviour:**

- Identify eligible TVET institutions.
- Display verified programmes for each institution.
- Display the institution's permitted residential options.
- Enforce the applicable selection requirements.
- Do not assume that every TVET institution offers both Day and Boarding.
- Do not automatically apply or remove general category restrictions without verifying how the official rules govern the pure TVET pathway.

---

## 5.2 STEM and Science Selection

### Official Guideline 14: STEM/Science Career Interest

Candidates interested in a STEM or Science career are instructed to select STEM/Science subjects in all five choices from Categories A, B, and C as Day or Boarding.

### Explanation

Candidates following this pathway should be able to identify schools and programmes that meet the applicable STEM or Science requirements.

The application must not treat every school as a STEM school simply because it offers a Science-related subject. Eligibility should be based on verified information from the official source.

**Application behaviour:**

- Identify verified STEM schools and schools offering relevant STEM subjects.
- Present eligible options for each main choice.
- Verify the relationship between the school, programme, and applicable STEM or Science requirement.
- Respect the available residential options.
- Do not invent a STEM classification when the source does not provide one.
- Verify how the STEM/Science pathway interacts with the general category and residential requirements before enforcing any special interpretation.

# SECTION 6: OFFICIAL APPENDICES

The supplied 2025 guidelines refer to the following appendices.

### Official Guideline 15: Appendix 1

Appendix 1 provides details of Technical Institution (TI) programmes.

### Explanation

The application should use this information when helping candidates identify programmes offered by Technical Institutions.

Programme names and eligibility must be taken from the verified source.

---

### Official Guideline 16: Appendix 2

Appendix 2 provides details of the subjects of Technical Programmes offered in Senior High Technical Schools (SHTS).

### Explanation

This appendix should support the programme-selection experience for candidates choosing relevant Senior High Technical School programmes.

The application must distinguish these programme details from those for Technical Institutions where the official materials do so.

---

### Official Guideline 17: Appendix 3

Appendix 3 contains the alternative school choices.

Candidates must select one Category B alternative and one Category C alternative from the eligible schools listed there.

### Explanation

The application must not assume that every Category B or Category C school in the main school register is automatically eligible as an alternative.

Alternative eligibility must be verified against Appendix 3.

---

### Official Guideline 18: Appendix 4

Appendix 4 contains Special Education Needs (SEN) schools.

### Explanation

SEN information must be handled accurately and respectfully.

The application must not invent special-school eligibility rules or assume that the general selection rules apply identically to every SEN pathway without verification.

Any dedicated SEN selection process must follow the official requirements established for it.

---

### Official Guideline 19: Appendix 5

Appendix 5 contains STEM schools and Senior High Schools offering STEM subjects.

### Explanation

This appendix should support candidates who need to identify schools and subjects relevant to the STEM/Science pathway.

The application must distinguish schools specifically identified as STEM schools from schools offering particular STEM subjects, where the official source makes that distinction.

# SECTION 7: INFORMATION REQUIRED ON THE SELECTION FORM

### Official Guideline 20: Main Choice Information

The supplied school-selection form contains the following fields for each main choice:

1. Serial number.
2. School code and name.
3. School category.
4. Programme code and name.
5. Residential preference: Day or Boarding.

### Explanation

Candidates should be able to review all the information associated with each school choice before completing the form.

The application should populate school names, codes, categories, programmes, and residential options from verified records wherever possible.

It must not fabricate missing school codes, programme codes, categories, or programme names.

---

### Official Guideline 21: Alternative Choice Information

The form provides a separate section for the two alternative schools.

### Explanation

The completed form should clearly distinguish the five main choices from the two alternatives.

Alternative schools must be identifiable as such and must satisfy the applicable category and eligibility requirements.

---

### Official Guideline 22: Parent or Guardian Details

The supplied form includes fields for the parent or guardian's:

- Name.
- Contact information.
- Signature.
- Date.

### Explanation

The application should provide the appropriate fields when producing or previewing the selection form.

It should not invent personal information or automatically represent an unsigned form as signed.

---

### Official Guideline 23: Headteacher Details and Validation

The supplied form includes fields for the headteacher's:

- Contact information.
- School stamp.
- Signature.

### Explanation

The application should preserve the required fields in the form preview or output.

It must distinguish between information entered into the application and an actual signature, stamp, or validation completed by the relevant person.

---

### Official Guideline 24: Parent or Guardian Consent

The supplied materials require parent or guardian consent and headteacher validation.

### Explanation

The application should make these requirements visible to the candidate before the selection process is considered complete.

A digital preview must not falsely indicate that consent, a signature, a school stamp, or headteacher validation has already been obtained.

# SECTION 8: REVIEWING AND COMPLETING THE FORM

This section describes the intended use of the guidelines within the application. It is an application-design explanation, not an additional government rule.

## 8.1 Review the Five Main Choices

Candidates should be able to review:

- The order of their five main choices.
- Each school's name and code.
- The category of each school.
- The selected programme.
- The residential preference.

Candidates should be able to return to a previous choice and edit it before completing the review.

## 8.2 Review the Alternative Choices

Candidates should be able to confirm that:

- Two alternative schools have been selected.
- One alternative is Category B.
- One alternative is Category C.
- Both alternatives are eligible under Appendix 3.
- Neither alternative repeats a school in the five main choices.

## 8.3 Validate the Selection

Before showing the form as valid, the application should check all applicable rules supported by verified official information.

These checks should include:

- The required number of main choices.
- Category limits.
- Day and Boarding requirements.
- School and programme eligibility.
- Availability of the selected residential option.
- Alternative-school category and eligibility requirements.
- Duplicate school selections.
- Applicable TVET, STEM/Science, or SEN pathway requirements.

The application must explain validation errors in clear language and identify which selection needs attention.

## 8.4 Preview the Completed Form

The final review should present the selected information in a form-like layout that is easy to compare with the official selection form.

Candidates should be able to identify mistakes and return to edit their choices before proceeding.

The preview must not claim that the form has been officially submitted, approved, signed, stamped, or accepted unless the relevant action has actually occurred.

# SECTION 9: IMPORTANT INTERPRETATION AND DATA-ACCURACY RULES

The following rules govern how the application uses this document. They are implementation safeguards, not additional government selection instructions.

### Rule 1: Official Text and Explanations Must Remain Separate

The application must visually distinguish official instructions from explanatory text.

Suitable labels include:

- **Official Guideline**
- **Explanation**
- **What this means for your selection**

The interface may use cards, expandable sections, a step-by-step reader, or other suitable presentation patterns.

Explanations must clarify the official instructions without changing their meaning.

### Rule 2: Do Not Invent School Categories

The category of a school must come from a verified official source.

If a school record has no reliable category information, the application must not guess its category.

### Rule 3: Do Not Invent Residential Options

The application must distinguish between:

- Day only.
- Boarding only.
- Both Day and Boarding.
- Unknown or unverified residential information.

Unknown information must not be treated as proof that either option is available.

### Rule 4: Do Not Assume All Schools Offer Every Programme

A programme must only be presented as an eligible option when supported by verified data.

### Rule 5: Verify Alternative Eligibility

A school must not automatically qualify as an alternative merely because it belongs to Category B or Category C. Appendix 3 must be checked.

### Rule 6: Handle Special Pathways Carefully

The general selection rules and the special TVET, STEM/Science, and SEN instructions must be examined together.

Where the supplied material does not clearly establish how a rule applies to a special pathway, the application must not silently invent an interpretation.

### Rule 7: Keep the Guidelines Maintainable

The guideline content should be stored separately from unrelated interface logic wherever practical.

This will make it easier to correct the content when an authoritative clarification is received or new annual guidelines are issued.

### Rule 8: Preserve the 2025 Context

This document describes the supplied 2025 selection materials.

It must not automatically be treated as the official guideline for a later selection year. New annual materials must be reviewed before the product presents them as current.
